Abstract

Traits ride along with distillation. A scheduled KL penalty removes most of them.

Distillation can transmit a teacher’s behavioral traits to a student through data that appears semantically unrelated to them — subliminal learning. We introduce liminal training, KL-regularized fine-tuning with an annealed regularizer. Across five open-weight models from 1.5B to 8B, it reduces mean trait acquisition from +3.32 to +0.85 while retaining nearly all of the task gain (+22.2 vs. +23.0 pp on GSM8K).

Method

KL-regularized fine-tuning with an annealed regularizer.

A KL penalty toward the frozen base model is added to the cross-entropy objective, weighted by a schedule over normalized training progress t. The schedule is what we study.

L(θ; t) = LCE(θ; D) + λKL(t) · LKL(θ0 ‖ θ)
Schedules

Early-weighted schedules sit on the Pareto frontier.

Liminal
Holds λ0 through the first epoch, then anneals linearly to zero.
Early anchor
λ0 for the first epoch, then off entirely.
Positive anneal
Ramps linearly from zero to λ0 across training.
End anchor
Off until the final epoch, then λ0 for that epoch.

Sweeping the strength of a constant penalty, task accuracy recovers first and saturates before trait acquisition does — relaxing further buys little accuracy while continuing to amplify the trait. Holding peak weight fixed and varying only the shape, liminal training and early anchor lie on or near the Pareto frontier, while positive anneal and end anchor fall below the constant-KL curve entirely.

Generality

The effect extends past preference traits to response style.

A gpt-4.1-nano teacher solves GSM8K entirely in French; the student trains on those traces and is then evaluated on English questions, never instructed to answer in French. Standard fine-tuning carries the style across along with the math. Liminal training holds the student at its base rate while still learning the task — and the timing pattern from the schedule ablations repeats.
